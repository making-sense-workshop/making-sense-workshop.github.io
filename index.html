About

Foundation models are reaching the senses. Wearables stream heart rhythms, motion and sleep from hundreds of millions of wrists. Microphones and earables capture speech, breathing and coughs. Tactile and olfactory sensors let robots and assistants feel and smell. Factories, vehicles and grids emit continuous telemetry. Sensor foundation models are now deployed to millions of users. Yet they are built in silos, one per application community, and the questions that decide whether multisensory intelligence works remain open.

How should heterogeneous sensors be aligned with language? How should missing data and device heterogeneity be handled at scale? Which sensors carry unique or synergistic information? Do sensor models reason or merely recognize? How can they be deployed safely on the body?

Making Sense brings the health-sensing, ubiquitous-computing, audio, robotics, industrial and time-series communities together at AAAI to establish how foundation models for multisensory intelligence should be built, evaluated and trusted.

Topics

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Wearable and biosignal foundation models (PPG, ECG, EEG, EMG)
  • Sensor-language models and reasoning agents
  • Tokenization and pretraining for heterogeneous sensors
  • Missing modalities, irregular sampling and device heterogeneity
  • Audio, tactile, olfactory and embodied sensing
  • Egocentric, ambient and industrial telemetry sensing
  • Multisensory fusion and transfer across speech, vision and sensor models
  • Benchmarks, datasets and evaluation of sensor reasoning
  • On-device, efficient, cost-aware and dynamic sensing
  • Personal-health agents, clinical validation, privacy and safety on the body

Call for papers

Submissions use the AAAI-27 template and are reviewed double-blind, so please anonymize your paper. We accept two kinds of paper.

Research papers

Novel methods, theory or experiments. Eligible for the Best Paper Award.

Demo and dataset papers

New datasets, benchmarks or demonstrable systems. Eligible for the Best Demo or Dataset Award.

Long papers
Up to 7 pages of technical content
Short papers
Up to 4 pages of technical content
References and appendices
Unlimited pages, for both lengths
Supplementary material
Allowed, but reviewers are not required to review it
Awards
Judged on the quality of the work, regardless of page length

Format

The workshop runs for half a day, with keynote talks, invited talks, oral presentations, a poster and live demo session, and a panel discussion. Speakers and the full schedule will be posted here.

Attending

There are no criteria to attend beyond AAAI-27 registration. Anyone interested in foundation models for sensor data and multisensory intelligence is welcome. The number of attendees depends on the size of the room.
